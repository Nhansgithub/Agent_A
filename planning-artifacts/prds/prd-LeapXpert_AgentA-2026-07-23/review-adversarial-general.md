# Adversarial Review — PRD-to-User-Document Automation Agent Flow

*Cynical pass. Assume the spec is hiding problems; attack hidden assumptions, unverifiable claims, gaps that bite in build, and internal contradictions. Professional tone, no softening. Fifteen findings.*

## Findings

- **[high] The whole product is human gates, and you only specified the gate opening.** FR-12 and FR-14 wait for a transition **to Done**. That is it. Real Jira workflows have Rejected, Won't Do, Duplicate, In Progress, reassignment, and cancellation. What does the agent do when the Head of Product moves the Publishing ticket to "Rejected" instead of "Done"? Nothing is written, so the agent will sit forever, or worse, misread some other transition. You wrote an entire §8 for edge cases — you handled "late feedback after Done" (EH-06) but not "the human said NO." That is not an edge case; that is the second-most-likely outcome of a review gate.

- **[high] There are no timeouts, reminders, or stall detection anywhere, in a product whose pitch is "documentation stops lagging."** Every gate is "wait for human." If the PM goes on vacation, the run parks in `awaiting_review` indefinitely with zero escalation. You built a system to fix the problem "documentation lags behind because it depends on a human's availability," and then made every single step depend on a human's availability with no backstop. The irony is not subtle.

- **[high] FR-03 asks an LLM to confirm a page is "genuinely a finalized PRD" and gives it nothing to decide with.** No definition, no examples, no accuracy bar, no test oracle. This gate decides whether the entire flow fires. A false positive drafts a public help doc from someone's half-baked notes; a false negative pesters a PM to rename a document that was already correct. "Genuinely" is carrying the entire feature and it is unfalsifiable. How does the builder test this? How does QA sign it off? They cannot, as written.

- **[high] FR-11 says the redraft loop repeats "with no cap until PASS" — on the same spec that will not stop talking about cost.** You locked a $6/mo box, you demanded LangSmith cost tracing (NFR-01), you wrote NFR-09 "Cost control … no runaway loops," and then you removed the only cap that matters. Yes, each round needs a PM comment, so it will not spin autonomously — but a fussy or confused PM can drive unbounded Claude spend across unbounded rounds, and nothing measures or bounds it. NFR-09's parenthetical only claims the *clarification/structure* loops are gated; it conspicuously does not mention the redraft loop. So which is it — "no runaway loops" or "no cap"? Pick one.

- **[medium] Idempotency (NFR-04) and the rename re-trigger (EH-04) are in direct tension and you waved at it instead of solving it.** NFR-04: "Dedupe by event/entity id." EH-04: a renamed page fires `page-updated`/`page-created` and must **re-enter** the flow at FR-02 — for the **same page id**. So your dedupe key cannot be the entity id, or the rename never reprocesses; but if it is the event id, a genuine duplicate delivery of that same `page-updated` double-processes. EH-04 asserts "Idempotency must ensure the earlier rename-request task does not cause duplicate processing" as if saying it makes it true. Nobody has said *how* dedupe-by-entity coexists with deliberate-reprocess-on-update. The builder will discover this the hard way.

- **[medium] Publishing moves the finished doc INTO the folder you are watching for new PRDs — hello, self-ingestion.** FR-15 moves the final UserDoc to the "main folder (for the demo: the same folder as the source PRD)." FR-01 fires `page-created` on that source folder. Moving/placing a page there plausibly emits an event that re-enters detection. The UserDoc title is not `final_PRD_...`, so FR-02 routes it to FR-02a — which then opens a **rename-request ticket nagging the PM about the document you just successfully published.** Every successful publish potentially spawns a spurious "please rename this" task. Nobody thought about what the watched folder does when you drop your own output into it.

- **[medium] "PM who created the page" quietly contradicts "PM fixed in config."** §4 says the PM is "Fixed in config." FR-02a assigns the rename task to "the **PM who created the page**." §13 Q4 admits this identity is scraped from the Confluence creator field. These are not the same person. If an engineer, a tech writer, or the Head of Product creates the page, FR-02a assigns the rename task to *them*, not the configured PM. And you are blithely assuming a Confluence account id maps cleanly to a Jira-assignable account id — cross-product identity mapping is a classic Atlassian footgun, not a given. Which PM wins when they disagree?

- **[medium] LangSmith is a "Must," and it means shipping confidential unreleased product plans to a third-party SaaS — unacknowledged.** NFR-01 mandates 100% LLM-call tracing in LangSmith. The payloads are PRDs and derived docs for **unreleased features**. That is product-confidential material egressing to an external observability vendor, and the PRD does not say one word about data governance, redaction, retention, or whether that is even allowed. "Observability" is not free of policy just because it is convenient.

- **[medium] The self-critique pass is asserted as a quality control and proves nothing.** FR-05: "draft → critique against skill file → single revision." Same model, one pass, against a SKILL.md that §9 admits does not exist yet and "need not be perfect." There is no measured improvement, no rejection threshold, no A/B. This is a ritual dressed as a control. If it does nothing, how would anyone in this demo ever know?

- **[medium] §§6.3–6.6 are missing and you did not notice.** The Functional Requirements section goes 6.0, 6.1, 6.2, then leaps to 6.7. Four subsection numbers vanished. Either you deleted requirements during the "adopted from `PRD_Agent_Flow.md`" excerpt and forgot to renumber, or there is content that never made it across. A build spec with holes in its own requirement numbering earns exactly zero benefit of the doubt about completeness.

- **[medium] "§Phase 5/6" points at nothing — the excerpt seam is showing.** §15.3 says treat OOM "during the end-to-end run (§Phase 5/6)" as the resize signal. There are no phases in this document. This is a fossil from the larger source doc that was pasted in without reconciliation. It is small, but it is proof the adoption was not proofread — so what *else* did not survive the copy?

- **[low] EH-02 resume is hand-waved at exactly the point where it is dangerous.** "Re-run the failed step from the last good checkpoint." What is a "step"? What is a "checkpoint"? §10 has a `last_good_checkpoint` field and §9 lists stages, but nothing connects them or defines granularity. FR-15 alone is four actions (lock, move, export, mark complete). Re-run it after it locked-and-moved but before export, and — unless every sub-action is idempotent, which is stated nowhere — you re-lock, re-move, or double-export. "Resume from checkpoint" is easy to write and hard to get right, and this is all write, no right.

- **[low] "Lock" is not a lock.** FR-15 applies Confluence page restrictions and calls it "functional equivalent of 'lock version'." Page restrictions govern *who may edit*, not *whether the content is frozen*. Space admins and the agent's own account retain edit rights; there is no version pinning. Calling it a lock oversells the guarantee and someone downstream will trust a freeze that does not exist.

- **[low] Your headline success metric is a sample size of one.** §3: "1 successful full run." A single happy-path execution declares victory — no error-path validation, no repeatability, nothing about the elaborate §8 edge handling actually working. Meanwhile §12's DoD is far more demanding (it lists error injection, resume, plain-language loop, etc.). Your success metric is weaker than your own Definition of Done, which means either the metric is lazy or the DoD is aspirational. Reconcile them.

- **[low] FR-04 cheerfully transitions tickets to Done without checking whether they are already Done or whether the transition is even legal.** "If found: SHALL transition it to Done." A ticket already in Done, or one whose workflow has no direct-to-Done transition from its current state (the exact risk §13 Q1 flags), will error or no-op. FR-04 assumes the transition always exists and always applies. §13 admits the assumption; FR-04 does not defend against it.
